    Grading ripeness, in order

    Ripeness is a scale, not a set of unrelated labels. We train a CNN with an ordinal-regression loss (CORAL) and compare it with a standard cross-entropy classifier on identical splits.

    Plate I — four grades of tomato and banana, from unripe to overripe.

    Plate II

    The problem with plain classes

    A grader that calls an unripe tomato overripe has made a far worse mistake than one that calls it turning. Ordinary multi-class training cannot see that: cross-entropy only asks “was the right box ticked?”, so every wrong answer costs the same.

    Ripeness grades have a natural order — Unripe < Turning < Ripe < Overripe — and an ordinal model uses it. It learns how far along the fruit is, so its mistakes tend to land next to the truth.

    Objectives

    • Build a 4-grade ripeness dataset for banana and tomato from Fruits-360, extended with a real-world banana subset and a workflow for self-collected photos.
    • Train the same ResNet-18 twice: with a cross-entropy head and with a CORAL ordinal head.
    • Compare them on identical splits with accuracy and MAE, plus severe-error rate and quadratic-weighted kappa.
    • Ship a working demo that grades a photo with both models.
    What a mistake costs

    Cross-entropy

    Every error costs 1.

    Ordinal

    Cost grows with distance |true − predicted|.

    Two numbers to judge a grader

    Accuracy = share of images graded exactly right
    MAE = mean | true grade − predicted grade |

    MAE is measured in grades: 0.10 means the average prediction is a tenth of a grade off. Two models can have the same accuracy and very different MAE.

    Plate IV

    One backbone, two heads

    Both models share everything except the last layer and the loss: an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18, 112 × 112 inputs, AdamW (lr 3·10⁻⁴, 10× for the new head, cosine schedule), 8 epochs, batch 32, grade-balanced sampling, and the same augmentations, seeds and splits.

    Augmentation, with one rule

    Random crops, flips, ±20° rotation, brightness and contrast jitter — but no hue jitter. Colour is the ripeness signal; shifting the hue would turn a green tomato red and keep the label “Unripe”.

    Baseline · cross-entropy

    4 logits → softmax → pick the largest.

    L = − log py

    Grades are treated as unrelated categories.

    Ordinal · CORAL

    Three yes/no questions: is the grade > 0? > 1? > 2? All three share one weight vector w and differ only in a bias bk:

    P(y > k) = σ( w·x + bk )

    A “Ripe” label (grade 2) becomes the targets [1, 1, 0]. The loss is the sum of three binary cross-entropies, so predicting Overripe for a Turning fruit gets two questions wrong, not one.

    ŷ = Σk 1[ P(y > k) > 0.5 ]

    Sharing w keeps the answers rank-consistent: P(y>0) ≥ P(y>1) ≥ P(y>2), so the model can never say “not past Turning, but past Ripe”.

    Plate IX

    Field notes

    Limitations

    • Fruits-360 frames come from a few physical fruits each; “seen variety” scores are optimistic.
    • Grades come from folder and caption rules, not from a sugar or firmness measurement.
    • No green banana exists in either public source, so these models have never seen an unripe banana and no self-collected photos are included yet.
    • Validation frames come from the same fruits as training, so checkpoint selection cannot detect poor generalisation to new cultivars.
    • The two sources label spotted bananas differently (some “Ripe” phone photos look like “Overripe” studio ones).

    Next steps

    • Collect 50+ photos per fruit and grade under mixed light; re-run run_experiments.sh.
    • Try CORN (conditional ordinal) and a fruit-type head; calibrate the score against days-to-ripe.
    Add to the sketchbook

    Self-collected subset

    Photograph a fruit, pick its grade, and save it. Photos land in data/self_collected/<fruit>/<grade>/ and join the next training run (70% train, 15% val, 15% real-world test).

    Labelling guide — Unripe: no yellow/red at all. Turning: colour change has started but green remains. Ripe: full colour, firm. Overripe: dark patches, wrinkles, rot or many brown spots.
